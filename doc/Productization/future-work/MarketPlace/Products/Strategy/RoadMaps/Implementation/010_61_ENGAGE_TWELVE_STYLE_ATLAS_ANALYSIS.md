## 1. The Correction In Direction

The first proposals were three sensible arrangements of essentially one visual
language. They varied content allocation more than brand character. That is not
enough variety for businesses that should look premium, expressive, personal,
editorial or technical, rather than all resembling the same SaaS dashboard.

The better answer is **a curated collection of visual identities**, independent
of what the workspace does. We can be much more expressive without inventing new
functions, changing AI behaviour or creating an industry-specific widget fork.

Twelve is a useful exploration set, not a claim that twelve styles cover every
business or every user's preferences. The business matches below are design
hypotheses, not market-research findings or regulated-sector qualifications.

### What Makes A Style Genuinely Different

Changing one brand colour is not a new style. Each preset should have a distinct
combination of at least four of these visual axes:

- Typography: editorial contrast, geometric confidence, humanist warmth, mono precision.
- Shape: architectural edges, tailored corners, softly rounded surfaces.
- Material: matte, paper-like, translucent peripheral chrome, flat colour blocks.
- Contrast: monochrome restraint, dark luxury, strong expressive accent.
- Density/rhythm: compact utility versus comfortable conversational reading.
- Boundaries: fine rules, strong outlines, unframed media, modest elevation.
- Message treatment: unboxed answers, softly framed responses, ruled editorial text.
- Control treatment: neutral tools, solid send affordances, clear but subtle states.
- Media treatment: edge-to-edge imagery or framed catalogue cards; no recolouring facts.

The prototype uses real locally bundled typefaces, not just names of fonts that
are not installed. It compares the same Northfield content and controls across
all twelve styles; changing sample category is a separate explicit review control.

## 2. The Twelve Directions

### 01. Clarity

**Character:** precise, familiar, capable.
White and cool neutral surfaces, cobalt focus, Manrope typography, crisp 4px
controls and modest elevation. The assistant feels like a well-made business
product rather than a decorative chat overlay.

**Could suit:** SaaS, business services, general-purpose customer assistance.
**Avoid:** allowing neutrality to become generic; customer identity still matters.

### 02. Editorial

**Character:** thoughtful, articulate, informed.
Instrument Serif identity and answer typography, red-ink emphasis, fine rules
and a generous reading rhythm. Inputs and operational controls retain legible
sans-serif labels. It feels like a well-edited advice column, not a dashboard.

**Could suit:** professional advice, education, publishers, research resources.
**Avoid:** decorative serif labels on dense forms or text sizes that crowd the dock.

### 03. Obsidian

**Character:** private, composed, premium.
Near-black matte surfaces, ivory type, restrained metallic accents and tailored
hairlines. Product photographs remain correctly lit and unfiltered; the premium
effect comes from typography and surrounding surfaces, not hiding the product.

**Could suit:** premium automotive, luxury retail, property and hospitality.
**Avoid:** low-contrast gold copy, large glowing decorations or fake exclusivity.

### 04. Botanical

**Character:** calm, grounded, considerate.
Chalk-white and forest-green surfaces, comfortable line spacing, quiet edges,
and subtly shaped response panels. A natural character without leaf decorations,
floating blobs or a beige-dominated interface.

**Could suit:** sustainable retail, home, wellbeing and community services.
**Avoid:** pale green text on green backgrounds; styling is not medical expertise.

### 05. Atelier

**Character:** cultured, individual, design-led.
Sculptural serif identity, a lacquer-like dark red header, blush-white reading
surfaces and precise asymmetric accents. More art-directed than Editorial:
strong brand framing, while the answer and controls remain practical.

**Could suit:** fashion, interiors, creative studios and boutique services.
**Avoid:** presenting an action confirmation as a fashion advertisement; clarity wins.

### 06. Kinetic

**Character:** energetic, decisive, contemporary.
Graphite, acid-lime highlights, bold Space Grotesk typography, cut corners and
strong visual anchors around input and selected scope. Energy comes from graphic
contrast, not perpetual animation or urgency tricks.

**Could suit:** mobility, performance retail, technology and active-lifestyle brands.
**Avoid:** flashing, fake progress, or exciting success animations around real writes.

### 07. Frost

**Character:** airy, polished, light.
Glacial neutrals, precise cyan, floating peripheral chrome and soft elevation.
Main answers, source text, inputs and confirmations stay opaque and readable.
An opaque fallback is part of the concept, not a separate degraded design.

**Could suit:** travel, hospitality, modern service brands and consumer apps.
**Avoid:** glass over arbitrary website text or depending on transparency for usability.

### 08. Workshop

**Character:** practical, tangible, dependable.
Structural black rules, yellow action accents, instrument-like mono labels,
square corners and a dark composer. It feels like a useful service instrument
rather than another pastel business app.

**Could suit:** manufacturing, fleet services, equipment and field support.
**Avoid:** treating yellow branding as a warning signal; statuses retain their meaning.

### 09. Terminal

**Character:** technical, focused, transparent.
IBM Plex Mono, dark neutral surfaces, phosphor accents and precise boundaries.
It is still the same ordinary AI conversation: not an actual shell, command
executor, raw debug console or permission to reveal internal model reasoning.

**Could suit:** developer tools, technical documentation and technical support.
**Avoid:** fake terminal activity, fake logs or simulated intelligence theatre.

### 10. Pop

**Character:** friendly, optimistic, memorable.
Nunito, bold outlines, short offset shadows, approachable corners and restrained
colour blocking. Graphic character is deliberate, without adding a mascot,
game-like controls or the impression that business actions are toys.

**Could suit:** learning products, creative retail and friendly consumer services.
**Avoid:** excessive colour, playful errors or celebratory confetti for writes.

### 11. Gallery

**Character:** confident, visual, understated.
Black-and-white restraint, unframed imagery, thin icons, open space and ruled
composer boundaries. Visual assets and customer content carry the identity;
there are fewer decorative containers, not fewer functions.

**Could suit:** design retail, architecture, property and visual catalogues.
**Avoid:** hiding pending/error/source boundaries in pursuit of minimalism.

### 12. Human

**Character:** warm, reassuring, personal.
Coral identity, friendly typography, softly framed messages and clear human
presence where enabled. More conversational than Botanical and less graphic
than Pop. Agent images and labels are existing identity presentation, not
permission to fabricate a team member or their availability.

**Could suit:** support, local businesses, member services and assisted sales.
**Avoid:** manufactured empathy, hidden AI identity or invented agent availability.

## 3. Style Is Not Business Logic Or Layout

The configuration dimensions must remain independent:

1. **Experience pack:** existing domain capabilities and result renderers.
2. **Surface/layout:** existing dock, Max, standalone and compatible panel arrangements.
3. **Visual style:** one of these twelve identities.
4. **Colour-scheme policy:** light, dark or follow system, separately from style.
5. **Customer branding:** logo, name and reviewed colour/type overrides.
6. **AI configuration:** modes, prompts, retrieval, actions and auth; untouched by style.

Every style has light and dark proposals. Obsidian/Kinetic/Terminal have a dark
default character, not a restriction that prevents a light version. Do not count
automatic system selection as another designed palette.

Do not automatically switch the pack, tool set, fields, result content or AI mode
when a style changes. A dealer can use Editorial or Botanical; a support workspace
can use Kinetic or Obsidian. Kit recommendations are starting points, not locks.
Do not infer style from protected demographics or present the suggested business
matches as a validated segmentation model.

## 4. Adapting To The Three Surfaces

### Docked Input

The dock should carry the smallest recognisable version of the identity: logo,
typeface, material, boundary and send affordance. Keep it dismissible and compact.
Do not add a tool shelf, large welcome block or permanent mode badges merely to
make a theme look different. An expressive header must scale down, not overwhelm
the website. Attached-page capture must keep its existing no-auto-open behaviour.

### Max Mode

Use the larger canvas for expressive heading hierarchy, message surfaces,
product imagery, evidence and existing context panels. The selected layout
continues deciding content allocation. Styles must not duplicate results or
invent an expanded workspace feature absent from the actual installation.

### Standalone

Carry the same identity through existing app chrome, navigation, sign-in and
history. Do not turn it into a marketing landing page. Authenticated personal
state, request navigation and agent controls only appear when actually provided.
The same app/deployment assignment stays intact when appearance changes.

### Phone / Narrow Width

Preserve identity with typography, colour and component treatment, not by
shrinking a desktop interface into unreadability. Reduce large identity text;
stack existing content; maintain reachable controls and safe-area behaviour.
Desktop remains primary. This does not add a mobile app or certification programme.

### Different Reading And Language Needs

Style is not demographic targeting. Preserve the host's configured language and
direction; do not infer them from theme choice. New typefaces need script-aware
fallbacks and actual Arabic/RTL and non-Latin checks before production adoption.
The Latin-script atlas does not establish that qualification. Keep comfortable
text sizing and high-contrast alternatives available without requiring a different
experience pack, and avoid making technical precision mean tiny text.

## 5. Where Creativity Should Be Concentrated

- **A recognisable dock signature:** concise identity and input treatment, no feature clutter.
- **Expressive type roles:** memorable identity/headings with legible forms and long answers.
- **Typed results that belong to the brand:** existing image/card/table/receipt renderers inherit tokens.
- **Material coherence:** one consistent visual story across header, composer and source panel.
- **Human/AI identity clarity:** real identities remain unmistakable in every aesthetic.
- **State coherence:** loading, unavailable, pending, confirmation, rejected and successful stay honest.
- **Purposeful microinteraction:** brief focus/press transitions; reduced motion respected, no semantic theatre.

The goal is not a new animation on every button. It is that a customer can
recognise their brand even after the logo is removed, without losing familiar
interaction patterns or trusting a false success state.

## 6. Implementation Implications, Not Implementation Approval

These twelve skins demonstrate the variation can be expressed mostly through
shared tokens and small, bounded component style recipes. This is not a proposal
for twelve React apps, twelve backends, or twelve copies of conversation state.

The existing widget still contains fixed colours, fonts and shapes. Production
work requires aligning those primitives and pack renderers; adding a preset name
to Console alone would not theme the actual installed experience completely.

Use a reviewed style identifier with semantic tokens, not arbitrary customer CSS
or remote executable theme code. Tokens cover surface/on-surface, accent/on-accent,
muted text, borders, shape, elevation, type roles, reading rhythm and icon weight.
Roles matter because a single primary colour cannot safely style every state;
this follows the established role/token approach described by
[Material Design](https://m3.material.io/styles/color/the-color-system).

Fonts should be approved, licensed and self-hosted in immutable widget assets,
with reliable fallbacks. The review bundles five Google Fonts families and their
OFL notices; future production asset delivery/CSP and font loading still require
qualification. Long answers and dense forms may use different roles, consistent
with [Material typography roles](https://m3.material.io/styles/typography/applying-type).

Minimum text contrast is a guardrail, not a claim that the prototype is fully
accessible. Normal text generally needs 4.5:1; large text has a 3:1 threshold.
See [W3C contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).
Test the actual adjacent surfaces, including tinted answers and disabled/error
states; a page-level score alone is insufficient.

Core invariants remain identical: authentication, runtime assignment, tools,
action parameters, required fields, confirmation, permissions, retrieval,
citations, attachments, navigation continuity, conversations and handover state.
Theme changes must not remount/reset a live conversation or require an AI runtime
rebuild. Publishing reviewed frontend assets is a separate concern.

Console should offer recognisable visual thumbnails and the current isolated
preview, with type/material descriptions and the three surface toggles. No list
of unexplained colour names, and no implication that selecting a style enables
Desk or changes the app's configured capabilities.

## 7. My Recommendation

Keep all twelve in the review set. They cover meaningful aesthetic territory
without claiming universal coverage. Suggested starting points:

- Broad business/default: Clarity, with Gallery for a more visual/understated brand.
- Premium dealership: Obsidian; a younger performance identity could use Kinetic.
- Human-centred Support: Human; technical Support could use Workshop or Terminal.
- Knowledge-led brand: Editorial; creative consultation could use Atelier.
- Calm consumer experience: Botanical; airy hospitality could use Frost.
- Expressive friendly brand: Pop, without weakening clear states and confirmations.

These are recommendations, not Kit restrictions. Prioritise implementation by
the actual brands/customer feedback, not by a promise to launch an enormous
theme editor. Qualify shared primitives first, then every approved recipe. A
style-only change should have a much smaller regression scope than an AI change.
