Why Shopping Companion > Cart Assistant
The Behavioral Truth
Shoppers do not hand their wallet to AI. They research, compare, question, then buy themselves. Every conversion study on e-commerce AI confirms this:

Users love AI review summarization
Users love AI comparison help
Users love AI "is this right for me?" Q&A
Users tolerate AI order tracking
Users distrust AI making purchases for them
The entire "agentic commerce" narrative has been confused on this. Everyone is building agents that transact. The real user demand is agents that help users decide.

The Trust Model Inverts
Dimension	Cart Assistant	Shopping Companion
Trust required	High — user gives AI authority to act	Low — user stays in control
Write operations	Many (add, remove, checkout, refund)	Few to none (maybe save wishlist)
Failure cost	High — wrong order, wrong refund, merchant liability	Low — at worst, bad recommendation
Governance burden	Heavy confirmation flows everywhere	Minimal — reads don't need guards
Merchant anxiety	"What if it places wrong orders?"	"Great, more informed buyers"
Positioning feel	Pushy sales floor	Knowledgeable friend
Shopping companion is the same platform, easier to trust, faster to adopt, less risky to ship.

What You'd Cut
Remove complexity that isn't needed for a companion:

Skip complex write flows (no refunds, no returns, no checkout from chat)
Skip Deep Resolver / ReAct for launch (reads + one-shot answers are enough)
Skip Admin API write scopes on Shopify OAuth (read-only scopes are faster to approve)
Skip confirmation-heavy policy engine usage (reads don't need it)
Every one of these cuts also accelerates Shopify App Store approval. Read-only apps get reviewed faster, have fewer privacy concerns, and need less scrutiny.

What You'd Add
Small additions that sharpen the companion fit:

Review ingestion as a knowledge source — Shopify has review apps (Yotpo, Judge.me, Loox). Ingest reviews as a separate vector space alongside products.

Policy knowledge — returns, shipping, warranty, size guides vectorized as a knowledge source. Answers to "what's your return policy?" with attribution.

Comparison card component — a new card type in the shell that shows two or three products side-by-side with highlighted differences. This is the one net-new UI element.

Companion-flavored system prompt — "You are a knowledgeable friend helping a shopper explore this store. You help them find, compare, and understand products. You never pressure them to buy. You tell them honestly when something might not fit their needs."

Read-only action set (replace the e-commerce write actions):

search_products (semantic + filters)
get_product_details (full spec dump)
get_product_reviews (summarized with sentiment)
compare_products (structured diff)
check_availability (by size, variant, location)

find_similar (discover alternatives)
get_size_guide (fit questions)
Notice what's missing: no add_to_cart, no apply_coupon, no cancel_order. The widget still has the Shopify cart integration for when the user clicks "add to cart" on a product card — but the AI doesn't initiate it. The user does.

The Honest Downside
Being a companion instead of an assistant means:

No direct conversion metric. You can't say "we placed $X in orders." You say "we helped users find the right product." Merchants need proof this translates to conversion.

Indirect revenue proof. You need attribution — "users who talked to the companion converted at 2.4x the baseline." This requires analytics instrumentation from day one.

No upsell capture. The companion won't push cross-sells. That's a feature, but some merchants will want the upsell bot anyway. Let them go to Rebuy.

Longer value story. "It answered policy questions better" is subtler than "it placed 400 orders." You need merchant case studies and testimonials.

You'll be asked "can it also do checkout?" repeatedly. You'll need a clear answer: "Not by design. The companion helps the shopper decide. The shopper checks out." Hold the line.

Recommendation: standalone Shopify app first, white-label second. Same runtime underneath.

Here's the reasoning.

Why standalone first

Distribution. Shopify App Store is the only free, high-intent distribution channel you'll get as an unknown brand. A white-label product has no shelf — you have to go sell it. Every hour spent on outbound sales is an hour not spent building. The App Store does the selling for you if the listing is right.

Validation before abstraction. You don't yet know which features merchants actually pay for. Shipping a standalone app forces you to pick defaults and live with them. Those defaults become the data you need before designing a configurable white-label product. Building white-label first means designing flexibility for use cases you haven't validated — the classic over-engineering trap.

Brand gravity. "Loom Companion, 4.8 stars on Shopify App Store, 500 installs" is a credential you can point every future enterprise customer at. "We have a platform, here's a demo" is not. The standalone product is the case study that sells the platform later.

Review velocity. A single opinionated app clears Shopify review faster than a platform that needs to explain every configurable surface. Read-only shopping companion with fixed scopes is the fastest possible approval path.

Cost to revisit is low. The platform already does the hard work. The app is a thin merchant-facing skin over Platform-V5. If it works, you layer white-label on top. If it doesn't, you've lost the skin, not the platform.

Why white-label second, not never

The platform is the long-term business. Shopify App Store revenue caps out at "good SaaS." Platform revenue (enterprise, multi-vertical, Smart Brain eventually) is where the real ceiling lives. The standalone app is the beachhead, not the destination.

Concrete shape

Phase 1 (now, ~9 weeks): Ship Loom Companion on the Shopify App Store. One SKU, opinionated defaults, read-only, no configuration beyond branding and tone. Fixed action set: product search, comparison, reviews, policy Q&A, attach-to-conversation. Free tier + paid tier based on conversation volume.
Phase 2 (after 50–100 installs): Add Loom Companion Pro — same app, unlocks a second tier where merchants can customize prompts, add custom knowledge sources, configure quick-actions. Still single-app listing, still opinionated, but with levers.
Phase 3 (after repeated "can I do X" requests): Open the Loom AI Platform publicly. Shopify becomes one of several connectors. Merchants who outgrew Companion Pro migrate up. Everyone else stays on Companion.
Same runtime, same marketplace, same platform. Three different packagings of it, shipped in the order that minimizes risk and maximizes learning.
